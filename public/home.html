His story

From two hubcaps to a whole wall

Every collection starts somewhere. His started in the driveway.

2022
Fall · the first ones

Two hubcaps in the driveway

The first two pieces were plain wheel center caps. He held them up like trophies. From then on, every parking lot was a treasure hunt, and he could name a car from its badge before he could read the word on it.

2023
Winter · the first wall

A wall of his own

Badges needed a home, so his bedroom got one: a pine display with a cut-out spot for every logo and a sign that reads Matthew's Collection. Big emblems hung on hooks along the top. Within a year, it was full.

2023
December · out of room

Then it covered the bed

By December the wall couldn't hold them, so they went everywhere. Mercedes stars, Audi rings, Ford ovals and Lexus Ls covered the whole bed, sorted and lined up by Matthew himself.

2024
Spring · the shelves

He needed a garage, so to speak

The collection moved to rows of black shelves. Badges stood up by make, BMW roundels in one row and script names like Regal and Thunderbird in another. Whole grilles started coming home too.

2025
All year · three walls

Three walls, floor to ceiling

Kia, Hyundai, GMC, Econoline, Lincoln, Cadillac. The shelves wrapped around the room. This was the year he filmed his own video tour and started asking for the dream badges by name.

2026
Now · taking it on the road

His favorites go to the car shows

Today the wall is the size of a room. For auto shows, Matthew built a travel board of his favorites: Porsche, Maserati, BMW, Renault and the big Isuzu across the top. He carries it past supercars he's still hoping to get a badge from.

The hunt

Where the badges come from

None of them came from a store. Every badge has a story about where it was found.

U-Pull yards

Cars heading for the crusher still wear their badges. A trip to pull one is a special-occasion treat, saved for birthdays and big wins.

Dealers & service shops

Parts departments swap out damaged emblems all the time. Matthew walks up, explains his collection, and asks. Plenty of people have said yes.

Kind strangers

At a Porsche dealer, the team talked cars with Matthew and sent him home with several hubcaps. At a Harley-Davidson shop, a detailer heard Matthew ask and ran to get two badges from his own collection. We wrote to Porsche's leadership and to the Harley shop's owner to thank them, and the detailer was named employee of the month.

House rules

The rules of the hunt

  1. If we pull over, it comes with us.Stop for a hubcap or a car part on the roadside and we take it, even if it's cracked or rusty. A badge hunt also cleans up the road.
  2. U-Pull is for special occasions.Dad takes him to the junkyard as a treat. Most badges have to be found some other way.
  3. Matthew does the asking.When we visit a business, Matthew asks for himself and explains his project. Nobody asks for him.
